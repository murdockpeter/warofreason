# Maurice / Fontenoy: simultaneous operational play

The application remains a Maurice campaign companion set around Fontenoy and the siege/relief of Tournai in 1745. C3i Waterloo is a reference for operational mechanics, not the campaign setting. The earlier Crossroads/Waterloo-flavoured prototype is retained only for save compatibility and regression tests.

## Create or carry over a Fontenoy setup

- **New campaign** retains the existing Maurice Fontenoy setup and full established campaign workflow.
- **New simultaneous Fontenoy campaign** creates the new operational workflow with the same sourced Maurice roster: French 20 and Allied 18, including Dutch 7 and Anglo-Allied 11. Opening columns, the 1745 opening date, commanders, named locations and Tournai objectives come from the repository's Fontenoy module.
- **Create simultaneous Fontenoy copy**, available on a saved Fontenoy campaign, creates a fresh operational campaign from its saved columns, forces, map locations, configured edges and objectives. The original campaign and its history remain unchanged. Units receive fresh identities and opening readiness; this is a setup copy, not a migration of ongoing battles or aftermath. Separate stacked columns and assign all units before copying.

The new map retains the 12 × 8 Fontenoy schematic's location coordinates and labels, expressed as 96 hexes for the adapted operational rules. It does not assert verified 1745 cartography. No roads/rivers or map scale are invented when the saved setup leaves them unspecified. Six-hour AM/PM phases and travel/command charts are provisional campaign adaptations; their timing is a game convention rather than a historical march-rate claim.

The hex map includes an original generated period backdrop: engraved watercolour fields, Flemish villages and fortifications on parchment, evoking 1745. Transparent hex shading and outlined labels keep operational information readable. **Show period backdrop** switches to the plain map when preferred. Painted lanes, buildings and waterways are decorative; configured hex data and road edges determine play. The asset and generation prompt are in `assets/maps/fontenoy-1745-operational-backdrop-v1.*` and ship locally for offline use.

## Guided play (0.10.1)

Leave **Helper mode** checked in the top bar for a live **What next?** panel. It follows your current view, the umpire's selected planning side, saved locks, preparation, contact/battle stage, siege review and any unfinished editor. It explains why a step is waiting and offers only a few immediate choices. **Show me** navigates to the screen, opens collapsed areas and highlights/focuses the relevant control; it does not choose or confirm an action. For movement it focuses a friendly counter: select it yourself, then click adjacent hexes and save the segment. Hold is a valid empty submission. Both sides must lock before the umpire resolves.

Guidance never switches faction views or discloses enemy orders/rosters. Shared records still require an explicit umpire view. Current edits take priority and remain intact through guidance/manual navigation. Turn the helper off/on in the top bar; its preference is stored locally for the app profile, separately from campaign data and backups. The manual remains available through Help/F1 or **Read the player manual**.

## Operational controls

Select French or Allied planning in umpire view. Select a column, choose adjacent hexes and save up to three timed movement segments. Fontenoy defaults to off-road/mixed travel because its base setup has no verified road edges. Lock both sides, then review/confirm simultaneous movement. Stand-offs and tactical battles pause required adjudication. Own-side control, influence and HQ command overlays help with routes; HQ names/affiliations are Maurice de Saxe, Cumberland and a generic Dutch command marker.

Optional **Enable preparation rules** adds ordinary screens and simultaneous withdrawals. Review HQ placement, due returns and charts first; deploy/recall screens before withdrawal submissions. Each side locks retreats or empty holds; the umpire previews and confirms their shared resolution. Withdrawn columns hold for the phase, while other columns receive concurrent preparation credit. Screens are allocated from parent columns for tactical play; they are not an automatic addition to the 38-unit Maurice force.

Column inspection shows the mapped Maurice units, type, quality, contingent and assigned commanders. Battle declaration freezes actual committed unit/commander rosters; agreed reinforcements add their own frozen roster and arrival window. Printable HTML/PDF briefs retain those snapshots. The result editor defaults the tactical game to **Maurice**, and outcomes/positions/arrivals remain manually entered. Another tactical game's adjudication can still be used by agreement.

## Per-unit aftermath and recovery

1. During clear planning in umpire view, expand **Maurice unit recovery → Review and enable unit recovery**. Review the rebuilding strategy, regular/irregular/artillery variant, recovery delay and retired rest. Select the calendar conversion and **Confirm unit recovery policy**. Native confirmation defaults to cancellation. Existing campaigns are not enabled automatically, and earlier battles are not retrospectively changed.
2. One recovery turn initially uses the saved source schedule's days-per-turn: the usual two-day campaign cadence proposes **48 calendar hours**. This is an editable campaign adaptation, not one six-hour AM/PM phase or an official Maurice recovery rule. Delay starts at the **recorded battle end**; checkpoint timing can make actual readiness later than the due time. Changing policies does not recompute existing scheduled dates.
3. Declare/play the tabletop battle as usual. Future battle/reinforcement briefs freeze only ready mapped Maurice units, with their current quality and commanders. Earlier briefs retain their original snapshots.
4. In **Enter manual result**, record each frozen unit as survived, retired, broken or not engaged. **Mark participating units survived** is an explicit convenience for formations marked as participating; it does not mark unused reinforcements. Broken units require the policy's recovery/loss action. Manual-review strategy allows individual choices only within the allowed rebuilding variant. Optional final disruption, unit notes and each side's final Army Morale are stored as observations.
5. Inspect **Unit effects to review**, then review/confirm the manual result. Recording and **Approve campaign effects** do not yet alter units. Resuming simultaneous resolution applies the approved formation and unit consequences together at battle end, with an after-action record and replayable journal event. Cancellation/error preserves the result draft.
6. Broken regular rebuilding changes current quality to **conscript**, preserves opening quality for scenario validation, and marks the unit recovering. Artillery/irregular recovery never assigns regular quality. Lost units remain in the fixed 38-unit historical roster. Retired rest is optional and preserves quality. Tactical disruption and Army Morale never become permanent unit disruption or campaign exhaustion.
7. At a shared checkpoint, **Finish shared phase** processes units due by the resulting campaign clock, once. This can include the normal overnight jump to the next AM phase. Recovered units become ready at their rebuilt quality. A blown formation still needs its separate reviewed legal return placement and at least one ready unit; recovery never silently places or stacks a formation.

The formation-level disposition must agree with the unit review. A formation with no ready units cannot remain on the map as retained/retreated; review it as blown if it has recovering survivors, or eliminated when all units are explicitly lost. Calling a formation eliminated does not invent unit casualties. Existing operational status/return rules and per-unit readiness are distinct records.

The **Recovery queue** shows scheduled/processed dates and current quality. Faction views expose only their own queue and roster; policy editing and full tactical results remain umpire workflows. Confirmed results retain their unit observations and optional Army Morale under **Recorded Maurice unit observations**.

Result corrections restore the precise pre-application unit baseline, replace that battle's recovery schedules and apply a new result revision. They are allowed only while the existing last-effect/unchanged-battle-end guard says no later movement, phase finalization or policy change depends on the result. Earlier journal and result snapshots remain intact. Late corrections require reconciliation rather than silently overwriting campaign history.

Ordinary screens still use manually agreed abstract troop allocation. A screen does not duplicate its parent's 38-unit roster, and a screen-only result cannot silently eliminate named parent units. Structured detachment troop allocation remains on the TODO list.

## Tournai siege and campaign victory (0.10.0)

1. During clear planning in umpire view, open **Tournai > Siege rules and checkpoint history > Review and enable siege rules**. Review the progress increment/threshold, calendar interval, optional separate exhaustion threshold, deadline and conflict/deadline outcomes. Native confirmation defaults to cancellation. This is optional and prospective: no time before enablement is credited and existing progress is retained.
2. The proposed interval follows the source schedule: normally **48 eligible calendar hours per increment**, not one six-hour phase. The proposed deadline is the source schedule duration from campaign midnight (normally 23 May 1745 at 00:00 for 22 days beginning 1 May). The deadline is explicitly editable and bounded by the engine's existing 100-phase window, day 50 at 18:00. Unusually long source schedules propose that supported endpoint instead and require review.
3. Finish simultaneous movement and all contact/battle adjudication. **Finish shared phase** waits for the umpire's **Review siege checkpoint**. Attest investment, French supply and Allied relief for the entire displayed interval since the last finalized checkpoint. It includes any overnight gap. If conditions changed or are uncertain within the interval, do not attest a fully eligible interval: record it as unsupplied/uninvested and explain the decision. The app does not calculate supply lines, infer investment/relief from occupation, or resolve tactical combat.
4. Enter each side's current **campaign exhaustion total** independently, with a required explanation. These totals are never derived from Army Morale, unit losses, disruption or Epic Points. Review the live progress/victory preview. **Confirm siege review** records conditions only; cancellation/error preserves the draft. You can revise this pending review before finalization.
5. **Finish shared phase** applies the reviewed checkpoint once, in the same transaction as the phase boundary. Only invested, supplied and unrelieved intervals earn eligible calendar minutes. Whole intervals earn configured progress; leftover eligible minutes carry forward. Pauses retain prior progress and eligible minutes; relief earns no new time. Progress caps at 10,000. Changing cadence requires no carried eligible minutes; other policy changes are prospective and never recompute recorded checkpoints.
6. At the completed checkpoint clock (before the next-phase/overnight jump), evaluate supplied investment at the siege threshold, confirmed relief, enabled campaign exhaustion and the deadline. Opposing substantive conditions use the configured conflict outcome. The deadline fallback applies only if no substantive claim exists. No victory triggers during policy setup, result entry, approval or condition review.
7. A recorded campaign outcome retains that final checkpoint clock and locks further strategic commands. Due unit recovery can still process in that same final transaction, through the final clock; there is no extra planning phase. Review/export history or clone a fresh setup. A new clone of an enabled siege removes siege/recovery history and returns to zero progress/not started; the original remains intact. Existing older clones keep their prior behavior.

The compact Tournai panel shows current status/progress and the next action. Policies and finalized checkpoints stay behind **Siege rules and checkpoint history**. Each checkpoint freezes the exact policy, interval, conditions, exhaustion totals, explanation, progress before/after, carried eligible time, outcome reasons and timestamp. These are shared campaign decisions visible in faction views; unfinished umpire forms and editing controls are withheld. The full journal preserves policy/review revisions and receipts. Prior finalized conditions are not editable.

All siege, supply, relief, timing and victory behavior is a provisional campaign adaptation, not official Maurice mechanics. Scouting integration, operational arrivals and structured detachment allocation remain separate TODO work. Commander/Notable management and optional manual Epic Points are described below.

## Commanders and Notables (0.11.0)

1. In clear umpire planning, expand **Commanders & Notables** (or use the Helper mode choice), then **Enable commander management** and review the native confirmation. This preserves the exact current roster and attachments as a reviewed baseline for future setup clones. It does not change officers, card effects, units, HQ modes/ranges or tactical rules. Older saves remain unchanged until explicit enablement.
2. Expand an officer to inspect their status, assignment and private Notable reference. **Edit [name]** changes the name/reference; **Record status for [name]** records an explicitly resolved active, retired or lost status. **Record officer arrival** creates a new unassigned active campaign officer for the chosen faction. Add a required decision explanation, then **Confirm officer change** and confirm the native dialog. Cancel/error keeps your draft; navigation and Helper mode can return to it.
3. **Assign [name]** transfers or detaches an active officer. Choose Unassigned or a mapped non-eliminated formation belonging to the same faction. No distance/march, tactical card or cross-contingent bonus is invented. Transfers and enablement require clear planning before orders or preparation deployment/withdrawal submissions. Officer details, arrivals and statuses can also be recorded at a fully settled shared checkpoint before the siege review. Outstanding contacts, unapplied battles, archive or campaign conclusion block changes.
4. Lost and retired officers detach automatically. Restoring active status does not silently reattach them. Battle result entry/approval does not infer commander losses or retirements: after the result is applied and the shared checkpoint is settled, record the agreed officer outcome separately before siege review. Operational HQ markers and command ratings remain separately configured; officer status does not automatically modify them.
5. Later battle/reinforcement briefs snapshot the currently attached active officers. Earlier snapshots keep the original names and references. Shared HTML/PDF briefs print committed commander names; private reference text remains in the officer panel and full umpire backup, and is not added to printed briefs. A later officer command advances the dependency guard, preventing a prior applied battle result from being corrected across that decision.
6. **Officer decision history** preserves the exact before/after officer details, attachment, explanation, campaign clock and timestamp. Faction views show only their own officers/references/history and no editing controls; shared management remains an umpire action. Keep copyrighted card text in your own materials; references are personal reminders and no card effects are interpreted.
7. A fresh operational clone uses the officer baseline frozen at enablement, with fresh identities and those baseline attachments/statuses. It omits later arrivals and decision history. The source history stays intact. If you enable management in an ongoing campaign, its current reviewed roster becomes this baseline; the app does not reconstruct an earlier officer setup.

## Optional manual Epic Points (0.12.0)

1. During clear planning (before movement orders are saved/locked) or a fully settled checkpoint, expand **Epic Points** and **Enable manual Epic Points** in umpire view. Nothing is scored automatically, and past battles are not assigned zero. The panel and Helper mode offer an optional post-battle review; scoring never blocks phase finalization.
2. **Record battle Epic Points** selects a battle whose result effects have been applied. It binds the entry to that exact result revision; one entry covers both factions. A reviewed battle without an entry is reported separately. **Record campaign event points** creates a separately named manual event, with no invented scoring triggers.
3. Enter each side's points from your own Maurice materials, with a required explanation. **Blank means not recorded; 0 is an explicit zero.** Enter per-event values, not a running total. The application accepts whole values from 0 to 10,000 per side/entry; these are storage bounds, not a claim about published scoring limits. No Maurice formula or card effect is interpreted. Confirm the native default-Cancel dialog; cancellation/error preserves your draft, including blanks, and Helper mode can return to it after navigation.
4. **Correct [entry]** loads its latest version. Review the values/explanation and provide a separate correction reason. A correction appends a new version linked to the previous one. Totals use only the current version of each entry, so earlier versions are never counted twice. Clearing a mistaken value back to blank makes it unrecorded. Battle sources stay fixed; a campaign-event title can be corrected without changing its entry identity. Stale/no-op corrections and a second entry for the same battle are rejected.
5. Recorded totals are sums of known current entries, accompanied by missing-value counts and reviewed battles without an entry. With no recorded values, the total says **Not recorded**, even if ledger entries exist with blanks. These totals are not a claim that the campaign's scoring review is complete. Entries do not change units, HQs, Army Morale, operational exhaustion, siege progress or campaign victory.
6. Complete any tactical result corrections before entering linked points. Ledger decisions advance the campaign dependency guard, preventing older battle effects from being silently revised across scoring decisions. Points corrections do not reopen tactical adjudication. Enter final-phase points before concluding the campaign; concluded/archived campaigns remain read-only.
7. Umpire view shows event titles, both sides' values, explanations, correction reasons and version history. Faction views show their own current values/totals and generic battle/campaign labels; other-side values, named events, notes and full correction history remain withheld. Full umpire JSON backups retain the ledger and replayable receipts. Fresh setup clones remove all scoring history.

## Save compatibility

The recovery extension, per-result observations, siege campaign extension, commander management and manual Epic Points are optional fields in aggregate 3. Older campaigns/snapshots stay unchanged until explicit commands; SQL migration 001 and JSON export format 2 remain in use. Full backups include policies, schedules, result revisions and replayable commands. Import/restart validate those transitions. A fresh operational clone removes aftermath history and restores opening quality/readiness with new identities. Use version 0.9.0 or newer for recovery saves, 0.10.0 or newer once siege rules are enabled, 0.11.0 or newer once commander management is enabled, and 0.12.0 or newer for the manual Epic Points ledger.
